Korean alphabet is called Hangeul (한글)

The Korean alphabet, Hangeul, was created in 1443 by King Sejong the Great of the Joseon dynasty.King Sejong recognized that the existing writing system, primarily based on Chinese characters (Hanja), was difficult for the common people to learn. He aimed to create a more accessible and user-friendly script for his people.


  • 한 (han) means "Korean"
  • 글 (geul) means "letter" or "script"

So, Hangeul literally translates to "Korean letters" or "Korean script."

To effectively read Korean letters, you must first familiarize yourself with the basic vowels and consonants, which are then combined to create syllables.

Let's start with the basics

The Korean alphabet, "Hangeul", is a phonetic script, meaning each letter represents a specific sound.
Here's a breakdown of Korean vowels and consonant

Vowels

Begin by learning the 21 vowels. Focus on their shapes and sounds.


Basic Vowels

  • ㅏ (a): Pronounced like the "a" in "father."
  • ㅑ (ya): Sounds like the "ya" in "yacht."
  • ㅓ (eo): Similar to the "u" in "but" or a short "uh."
  • ㅕ (yeo): Pronounced as "yuh."
  • ㅗ (o): Like the "o" in "old."
  • ㅛ (yo): Sounds like the "yo" in "yogurt."
  • ㅜ (u): Resembles the "oo" in "moon."
  • ㅠ (yu): Pronounced as "yoo."
  • ㅡ (eu): A very short "uh" sound, or similar to the "eu" in "heard."
  • ㅣ (i): Pronounced as the "ee" in "tree."

Compound Vowels

  • ㅐ (ae): Pronounced as "ai" in "bait."
  • ㅒ (yae): Sounds like "yay."
  • ㅔ (e): Similar to "eh" in "bet."
  • ㅖ (ye): Pronounced as "yeh."
  • ㅘ (wa): Sounds like "wa" in "water."
  • ㅙ (wae): Pronounced as "way."
  • ㅚ (oe): Also pronounced as "way" or "we."/span>
  • ㅝ (wo): Sounds like "wo" or "wuh."
  • ㅞ (we): Pronounced as "we."
  • ㅟ (wi): Pronounced as "wee."
  • ㅢ (ui): Can sound like either "wee" or "oo-ee."
Consonants

Hangeul has 14 basic consonants. Focus on their shapes and sounds.


Basic Consonants

  • ㄱ (giyeok) - Pronounced as "g" or "k"
  • ㄴ (nieun) - Pronounced as "n"
  • ㄷ (digeut) - Pronounced as "d" or "t"
  • ㄹ (rieul) - Pronounced as "r" or "l"
  • ㅁ (mieum) - Pronounced as "m"
  • ㅂ (bieup) - Pronounced as "b" or "p"
  • ㅅ (si-ot) - Pronounced as "s" or "t"
  • ㅇ (ieung) - Can be silent or represent the "ng" sound
  • ㅈ (jieut) - Pronounced as "j" or "ch"
  • ㅊ (chieut) - Pronounced as "ch"
  • ㅋ (kieuk) - Pronounced as "k"
  • ㅌ (tieut) - Pronounced as "t"
  • ㅍ (pieup) - Pronounced as "p"
  • ㅎ (hieut) - Pronounced as "h"

Fortified Consonants (Double Consonants)

  • ㄲ (ssangiyeok) - Pronounced as "kk"
  • ㄸ (ssangdigeut) - Pronounced as "tt"
  • ㅃ (ssangbieup) - Pronounced as "pp"
  • ㅆ (ssangsiot) - Pronounced as "ss"
  • ㅉ (ssangjieut) - Pronounced as "jj"
Syllables

In Korean, vowels and consonants combine to form syllables.


Basic Structure

  • Consonant + Vowel: This is the most common structure.
  • Example: ㄱ + ㅏ = 가 (ga)
  • Consonant + Vowel + Consonant: Some syllables include a final consonant.
  • Example: ㄱ + ㅏ + ㄹ = 갈 (gal)

Combining Vowels

  • Compound Vowels: Two or more basic vowels can combine to form a single sound.
  • Example: ㅗ + ㅏ = ㅘ (wa)

Vertical Vowels

  • Vowels like ㅏ, ㅐ, ㅑ, ㅒ, ㅣ are positioned to the right of the consonant.

Horizontal Vowels

  • Vowels like ㅗ, ㅛ, ㅜ, ㅠ, ㅡ are positioned below the consonant.
